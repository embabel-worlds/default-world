# Reading permission and exception clauses

Contract drafting expresses permission through negation, so the surface reading of a
clause is often the opposite of its effect. Decide what a clause DOES before deciding
what it says.

## Clauses that GRANT rather than restrict

Wording of the form *"nothing in this Agreement shall prevent / restrict / preclude /
prohibit / limit"* or *"the Receiving Party shall not be prevented from"* **permits** the
activity that follows it. So does *"this Agreement does not apply to"* and *"the
obligations herein shall not extend to"*.

Read the whole sentence before answering. A permission is frequently attached to the end
of a restrictive sentence, and the restriction is the part that matches a keyword search:

> The Receiving Party shall hold the Confidential Information in strict confidence and
> shall not disclose it to any third party; **provided, however, that nothing herein
> shall prevent the Receiving Party from disclosing** Confidential Information required
> to be disclosed by law.

The answer to "may the recipient ever disclose it?" is yes, with a condition.

## Standard exceptions to confidentiality obligations

Most agreements exclude information that:

- was already known to the receiving party before disclosure
- is or becomes publicly available through no breach by the receiving party
- was independently developed without use of the disclosed information
- was received from a third party who was free to disclose it

Where such a clause exists, the information is **outside** the confidentiality obligation.
A question asking whether the agreement restricts, prohibits or covers such information is
answered by the exception, not by the general obligation.

## Answering

- Quote the operative words, including any *provided that* / *except* / *unless* rider.
  A quote cut before the rider changes the meaning.
- Say what is permitted or excluded, not merely that the topic is "addressed".
- Where an obligation and an exception both apply, state the obligation and the exception:
  the accurate answer is usually "restricted, except in these cases".
- If the agreement genuinely contains no such clause, say so — silence is not permission,
  and it is not prohibition either.
