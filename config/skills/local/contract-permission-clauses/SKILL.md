How contract language works, when a question turns on what an agreement allows or requires:

- Obligations, permissions and exceptions are different things. Answer the one asked.
- A sentence is not complete until its provisos are read. "Provided, however, that",
  "except", "unless" and "subject to" routinely reverse the clause they follow.
- Permission is often drafted as negated restriction: "nothing shall prevent X" allows X.
- Confidentiality duties normally carry exceptions — information already known, public,
  independently developed, or received from a third party — which place that information
  outside the duty rather than within it.
- Capitalised terms are defined terms; their contract meaning governs over ordinary usage.
- Where a general clause and a specific one both apply, the specific one governs.
